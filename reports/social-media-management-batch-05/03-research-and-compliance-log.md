# 03 — Research and Compliance Log (Batch 05)

Research ran 13 November 2026. Every regulatory statement in the articles was checked against the issuing body's own page or the official Gazette, except where this log says otherwise. Full source tables (URL, issuing body, document number, date, clause and access date) are in `sources/` (one file per article file).

**This is not legal advice.** The articles tell readers to confirm with their State Medical, Dental or Veterinary Council or a legal adviser wherever the rules are unclear. Kudozz should treat the "needs professional review" items below the same way.

## 2. Corrections made during research
- **Spec error:** the commissioned-photograph rule is s.17 proviso **(b)**, not (a). Article 19 cites (b).
- **Dental regulator:** the brief and the spec referred to the "Dental Council of India". Under the NDC Act 2023 it was dissolved on 19 March 2026. The articles say this. Checked: no existing page names the DCI; `dental-oral-care-influencer-marketing-india` refers generically to "the relevant dental council" or "state dental council", which is still accurate.

- Yoga Certification Board and Ministry of Ayush certification (official site refused connections). Article 12 says to state training exactly as on the certificate.

- No statistics are used in any article.

## 4. Needs professional review before publishing

### Medical (NMC): articles 05, 08, 10
1. **Before-and-after (05):** the safe policy is none on clinic or doctor accounts, and a clinic feed "is unlikely to qualify" for the 8.1(v) scientific or educational exception. This is our interpretation.
2. **Agency engagement (05, 08, 10; also 06):** the tension between 8.1(ii) and 8.1(viii) is left unresolved, and readers are told to ask their council. **Business decision for Kudozz:** whether to market social media management to doctors and clinics at all until an SMC or NMC clarification exists. The CTAs on these pages already tell readers to check first.
3. **Product mentions (05, 08):** an unpaid brand recommendation by a doctor is treated as barred (8.1(iv)). Confirm whether generic ingredient education is acceptable.
4. **Price posts (05, 10):** a plain fee list is allowed; discounts and countdown offers are not (8.1(x) proviso; IMC 6.1.1). Packages need legal confirmation.
5. **Doctors (08):** monetising an educational YouTube channel (5.1), a booking link in the bio, and a registration number on every post (Expl. III). All are framed as "check with your SMC".
6. **Diagnostics (10):**
   - whether a standalone lab is a "medical institution" (cl. 3.4);
   - rate display under CE Rules 9(i);
   - NABL 133 symbol use (read via the FAQ, not the 133 text);
   - specimen commissions (8.1(vii), IMC 6.4.2).

### Physiotherapy: article 09
7. The draft NCAHP ethics regulations are paraphrased as proposals. **Re-check the NCAHP regulations page before publishing**, as they may have been finalised since.
8. Titles and the "Dr" prefix need professional review.

### Dental: articles 06, 07
9. Treating the 2014 Code as operative under s.59(2) of the NDC Act (counsel to confirm).

13. "Consent does not change that reading" for before-and-after images and testimonials.
14. Product recommendations (8.15.5, 8.1.6).

### Veterinary: article 20

16. The DMR Act applied to pet medicine claims.

### Coaches, tutors, home services, photographers: articles 16–19

26. The Consumer Protection Act s.2(28) examples.
27. Electrical, gas and AC safety framing should be checked by a trade or safety reviewer.
28. The copyright proviso: "a paid wedding shoot may fall under" it.

### Salons and spas: articles 01–04
29. "Reduces stress" and "relieves back pain" are treated as health claims; the line is our interpretation.
30. ASCI's filter guidance is extended to a professional's own portfolio.
31. Instagram's "Book now" button needs a partner; this was confirmed only via an official-domain snippet.
32. Whether the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy binds users of the free app.

## 5. Privacy and consent rules applied in every article
- Written consent for anyone identifiable; separate guardian consent for minors.
- Never identify patients, students or pet owners without consent.
- No health data in posts or DMs.
- Patient and student details never go into AI tools.
- Reply templates move clinical, pet-health, result and complaint questions to a private channel and the qualified professional.
