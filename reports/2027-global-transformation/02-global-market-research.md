## Cross-market patterns (Strategic notes)
1. **Disclosure labels differ by market.** Briefs need a label list per market:
   - US: "sponsored" is acceptable.
   - UK: "Sponsored", "Spon" and "Sp" are insufficient.
   - Singapore: "#sp" is accepted.
   - Australia: "#sp", "gifted" and "collab" may not be enough.
   - France: "Publicité" or "Collaboration commerciale".
   - Germany: "Werbung" or "Anzeige".
2. **Licensing is the Gulf's defining feature.** The UAE (NMA permit), Saudi Arabia (Mawthooq), Oman (media licence) and Bahrain (licensed adverts) each license creators differently. This is Kudozz's clearest content advantage, as agencies rarely explain it (observed in search results).
3. **Agencies are increasingly in scope.** This applies to the FTC (US), France (shared liability), the EU Digital Fairness Act (proposed) and ASCI (shared responsibility). Kudozz's written compliance process is a credible differentiator.
4. **Children and minors:**
   - Australia: under-16 account restriction.
   - Malaysia: under-16 ban from 1 Jun 2026.
   - US: COPPA amendments.
   - India: DPDP verifiable parental consent.

   Youth-audience creator campaigns need an age-gate review in every market.
5. **Kudozz's credible international angles:**
   - Indian brands expanding abroad: the UAE (CEPA), the UK (FTA 15 Jul 2026), the GCC and Australia.
   - Diaspora audiences: 4.34M overseas Indians in the UAE (MEA); 1.86M people of Indian ethnicity in England and Wales.
   - Multilingual creator sourcing.
   - Written compliance per market.

## 2. Regulation

### 2.1 ASCI (self-regulatory; ASCI Code + guidelines)

- **ASCI Guidelines for Influencer Advertising in Digital Media.**
  - The consolidated PDF is signed 17 Aug 2023, and there is a 2025 reformatted PDF.
  - **Labels (1.3a):** Advertisement, Ad, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Employee, Free gift, Affiliate. Platform tools also count: Instagram "Paid Partnership" and YouTube "Includes Paid Promotion".
  - "#collab" is not a listed label.
  - Disclosure must be upfront and prominent, not in the bio and not buried in hashtags.
  - On-screen label duration: videos ≤15s, at least 3s. Videos of 15s–2min, one-third of the length. Videos of 2min+, the whole brand section.
  - Material connection includes free products "including those received unsolicited" and media barters.
  - Virtual influencers must disclose that they are not human (1.4).
  - Sources: https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/GUIDELINES-FOR-INFLUENCER-ADVERTISING-IN-DIGITAL-MEDIA.pdf ; https://www.ascionline.in/social/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ASCI-Influencer-Guidelines.pdf . VERIFIED-OFFICIAL (reused).
- **Addendum II update, 28 Apr 2025** (verified this session from the ASCI press release PDF):
  - Health and finance influencers now need qualifications, declared upfront, **only where technical information or advice is provided**.
  - Generic promotions or PSAs do not need them. ASCI's examples: an insurer promoting annual health check-ups, and a chef promoting a meal service.
  - Source: https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Press-Release_Influencer-Guidelines-Update.pdf. VERIFIED-OFFICIAL.
  - Separately, Addendum II requires BFSI influencers giving investment advice to be SEBI-registered and to display their registration number (reused).
- **LinkedIn advisory:** ASCI asked LinkedIn influencers to disclose material connections, since LinkedIn has no platform disclosure tool. Source: MediaNews4U, 14 Jan 2025. PRESS-REPORT.
- **ASCI Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of Synthetically Generated Content in Advertising:**
  - The guidelines PDF is dated 17 Sep 2026; the press release is **29 Sep 2026**. The draft was released 12 May 2026.
  - **Effective "three months from the date of publication"**, so ~late Dec 2026. The exact base date (17 vs 29 Sep) is unclear.
  - **Prohibited even with a label:** fabricated endorsements or testimonials; deepfakes or likeness without consent; synthetic exaggeration of results; fake locations shown as real; unauthorised copyrighted work.
  - **Label mandatory:** synthetic influencers and ambassadors; consented likeness or voice replication; fabricated events; demos of products that don't exist; paid AI product suggestions ("Sponsored by [Brand]").
  - **No label needed:** routine edits, obvious fantasy, and accessibility uses such as translation and subtitles.
  - Suggested labels: "Audio/Video created using AI" or "Audio/Video enhanced using AI".
  - Sources: https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Press-Release_Guidelines-for-Responsible-Labelling-of-Synthetically-Generated-Content-in-Advertising.pdf ; guidelines PDF in the same folder. VERIFIED-OFFICIAL. Status: ANNOUNCED, not yet in force on 10 Nov 2026.
- **ASCI Annual Complaints Report FY2025-26:**
  - 1,609 influencer ads were processed; **97.3% needed modification**.
  - Top violation categories: illegal betting 54%, personal care 16.9%, electronics 7.9%, F&B 6.3%.

### 2.2 CCPA / DoCA (Consumer Protection Act 2019)
- **CPA 2019 s.21 (bare text, verified this session via advocatekhoj)**:
  - **Penalty on manufacturer or endorser:** up to **₹10 lakh**, and up to **₹50 lakh** for every subsequent contravention [s.21(2)].
  - **Endorsement ban:** up to **1 year**, and up to **3 years** for subsequent contraventions [s.21(3)].
  - **Publisher or party to publication:** up to **₹10 lakh** [s.21(4)].
  - **Endorser due-diligence defence** [s.21(5)].
  - Source: https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/consumerprotection2019/21.php. VERIFIED-OFFICIAL (statute text via a bare-act mirror).
- **CPA s.89** (criminal: misleading ad by manufacturer or service provider): imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to ₹10 lakh; subsequent offence up to 5 years and ₹50 lakh. Seen only in law-firm summaries; the 2-year/₹10 lakh part is cited by Bar & Bench. LAW-FIRM. **Re-verify the bare text before publishing.**
- **"Endorsements Know-hows!"** (DoCA, **20 Jan 2023**):
  - Applies to celebrities, influencers and virtual influencers.
  - Disclose material connections in a way that is "clear, prominent and extremely hard to miss".
  - Labels suggested: advertisement / sponsored / collaboration / paid promotion.
  - In video, the label must be in both the video and the audio. Live streams need a continuous label.
  - Endorsers should have used the product.
  - The document sets no penalties itself; it relies on the CPA.
  - Source: Khaitan ERGO 31 Jan 2023 (https://khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2023-02/Ergo_31012023.pdf). LAW-FIRM. The government PDF (consumeraffairs.nic.in PIB1892527.pdf) refused connection, so the exact government wording is unverified.
- **CCPA Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements, 2022** (9 Jun 2022): LAW-FIRM (reused, Khaitan). Not re-fetched.
- **Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023**:
  - Issued by CCPA under CPA s.18 on **30 Nov 2023**.
  - **13 specified dark patterns** (Annexure I), including false urgency, basket sneaking, confirm shaming, subscription traps, bait and switch, drip pricing and **disguised advertisement**. The last is relevant to undisclosed influencer content.
  - Applies to platforms, advertisers and sellers.
  - **CCPA advisory of 5 Jun 2025:** e-commerce platforms must self-audit within 3 months and file self-declarations.
  - Sources: Storyboard18 (Lok Sabha reply), Bar & Bench, Fox Mandal. PRESS-REPORT/LAW-FIRM. The CCPA PDF was not opened.

### 2.3 DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025
- The Act was enacted **11 Aug 2023**. The **Rules were notified 14 Nov 2025** per the PIB backgrounder of 17 Nov 2025. Some law firms say 13 Nov 2025 (Gazette date vs PIB date).
  - The Rules give an **"eighteen-month period for phased compliance"**.
  - Every Data Fiduciary must issue a separate, clear consent notice.
  - Consent Managers must be India-based companies.
  - Breach: affected individuals must be told "without delay".
  - Rights requests must be answered within **90 days**.
  - **Children's data needs verifiable parental or guardian consent.**
  - The Data Protection Board is fully digital, with 4 members.
  - **Penalties:** up to **₹250 crore** for failing to maintain security safeguards; up to **₹200 crore** each for failing to notify a breach and for violating children's obligations; up to **₹50 crore** for other violations.
  - Source: https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf. VERIFIED-OFFICIAL (read this session).
- **Phasing** (LAW-FIRM consensus: AZB, Conventus, Mondaq; not in the PIB backgrounder):
  - **Immediate:** Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 (Board).
  - **+12 months (~13 Nov 2026):** Rule 4 (Consent Managers).
  - **+18 months (~13 May 2027):** Rules 3, 5–16, 22, 23 (notice, consent, security, breach, children's data and the other substantive duties).
- **Proposed acceleration:** in Jan 2026, MeitY consulted on cutting 18 months to 12 months, which would make the deadline ~13 Nov 2026. It also proposed a shorter window for Significant Data Fiduciaries. Comments were due 4 Feb 2026. **I found no notification adopting this as of the latest reports (Mar–Apr 2026).** Sources: Storyboard18, S.S. Rana, Mondaq, Takshashila. PRESS-REPORT/LAW-FIRM. **Status uncertain. Check MeitY before stating the deadline.**
- **Implications for brands and agencies (analysis, not law):**
  - Creator databases hold names, phone numbers, PAN, bank details and audience demographics. Consumer data collected through campaigns includes UGC contests, giveaways, lead forms and WhatsApp opt-ins.
  - Both need purpose-specific consent notices, retention limits, breach processes and a published contact. Contracts between brand and agency (Data Fiduciary and Processor) should be in place.
  - Contests involving under-18s need verifiable parental consent.

## 5. UK — regulation

### ASA/CAP + CMA: Influencers' guide, 4th edition (October 2026)
Source: https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html (page dated 23 Oct 2026). Full PDF read (cover "October 2026"): https://www.asa.org.uk/static/17cf70a4-f904-4684-a68c8310ba7700e1/Influencers-guide-to-making-clear-that-ads-are-ads.pdf. Label: **VERIFIED-OFFICIAL**.

- **Legal basis:** the main rules are **Part 4 of the DMCC Act 2024** (unfair commercial practices: fake reviews, concealed incentivised reviews, undisclosed paid advertorial, posing as a consumer, omitting material information) **plus the CAP Code** (s.2 recognisability, s.3 misleading).
- **Who counts as an influencer:** any human, **animal or virtual persona** on any platform.
- **When to disclose:** payment = money, **free products (requested or unsolicited, even with no obligation to post)**, trips, stays, loans, discounts, commission, shares, ambassador or director roles, own-brand content, and family or friends' brands.
- **Duration:** disclose through any ongoing relationship and **for 12 months after it ends**.
- **Affiliate content** (Amazon Associates, Awin, Skimlinks, LTK) is advertising. Brand and creator are both responsible. Avoid "aff", "af" and "afflink".
- **Recommended labels:** #Ad, #Advert, #Advertising, #Advertisement, #AdvertisementFeature. The "#" is optional if the label is prominent.
- **Labels to avoid:** Gifted, Affiliate, Collab, PR, "Supported by", "In partnership with", tags alone, and **"Sponsored/Spon/Sp"** (ruled insufficient).
- **Platform tools (CHANGED from 2023):** a platform's branded-content label "**may be sufficient if the label is upfront and prominent**". If it blends in, add your own. "Co-authoring" is not always enough.
- **Placement:** at the very start (post text, title, thumbnail or image), not behind "more" and not mid-video. Label each Story in a series and each format separately. For ad breaks in long-form content, label the ad section.
- **ASA research (Feb 2026):** 79% of UK people think labels should be visible straight away. 80% prefer influencers to be clear. In a feed test, only about half identified ads correctly.
- **ASA "control" test** (for CAP Code remit over paid content): if the creator "weren't completely free to do and say whatever [they] wanted… or to do nothing at all", control is probable. **Under consumer law, control doesn't matter.**
- **Sanctions:**
  - ASA: listing on the non-compliant influencer/advertiser page for up to 3 months, and on-platform targeted ads warning followers.
  - CMA and Trading Standards: CMA direct fines and directions under DMCC Part 4; both can also go to court.
- **New in this edition:** a fake-reviews section and an **AI note** ("AI-generated content must also comply; some platforms may require disclosure of AI use").
- Industry bodies named: IAB UK, the **Influencer Marketing Trade Body (IMTB)** and its Code of Conduct, and ISBA (Creator Marketing Forum, contract templates).
- Lewis Silkin commentary (23 Oct 2026), https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/23/new-influencer-guidance-from-the-asa-cap-and-cma-what-has-changed-and-what-to-d-102o6ar, confirms the main changes: DMCC basis, fake reviews, the Sponsored labels singled out, platform tools and AI. ### 8.3 What an agency without a UAE/KSA licence or office must not claim
Evidence base: NMA pages (VO), archived FAQ (VO-archived), Saudi press (PR).
- **Who holds the UAE permit:** the *individual* creator (resident permit) — not the brand or a foreign agency. A visitor creator's permit can only be applied for by a **UAE-licensed, regulator-accredited advertising/talent agency** (holding a UAE economic licence for advertising/talent management). An India-registered agency cannot apply for or "arrange" a visitor permit itself; it would need a UAE-licensed partner.
- **Brand/requester obligations** (archived FAQ Q105; Gulf News Feb 2026): contract only with permit holders in the public register, written agreement, ensure compliance, no unpermitted visitors. Foreign advertisers "must contract with authorized UAE nationals, residents, or visitors" (Q71).
- Do not claim: a UAE office, UAE licence, accreditation by NMA, "NMA-approved/licensed agency", ability to obtain permits, "permit-compliant guarantee", or that Indian creators can post sponsored content to UAE audiences without a UAE permit (Q72: permit needed if audience is in the UAE). Do not imply the agency can lawfully fly in creators to post in the UAE without a UAE-accredited agency.
- Abu Dhabi: ADDED licence may also apply for influencers/businesses there (2024 rule, status in 2026 unverified).
- Saudi: creators need Mawthooq; non-Saudis need licence/commercial entity; advertising agencies need their own GAMR licence (repo `gamrAgency`). Do not claim Saudi licensing or the ability to engage non-licensed visiting creators.
- Kuwait from ~Apr 2027: visitors targeting Kuwaiti audiences also need a licence (as reported).
- Safe framing (already used in repo): "we check permit status", "we work with permit-holding creators", "visitor permits are arranged through an accredited UAE agency", plus "not legal advice".

### 1.3 Digital Services Act (Reg. (EU) 2022/2065)
- **Art. 26(2)**: online platforms must provide a functionality for users to declare that content they provide "is or contains commercial communications". **Art. 26(3)**: platforms must make declared content identifiable to other users clearly, unambiguously and in real time, incl. prominent markings. ### 1.4 AVMSD (Directive 2010/13/EU as amended 2018/1808) — "vloggers"

### 1.5 GDPR basics (Reg. (EU) 2016/679)

- Labels (Art. 5): **"Publicité" or "Collaboration commerciale"**; **"Images retouchées"** for body/face-altering retouching; **"Images virtuelles"** for AI-generated faces/bodies.

### 2.4 Spain
- **Real Decreto 444/2024 (30 Apr 2024, in force 2 May 2024)** — https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2024-8716 (fetched) **VERIFIED-OFFICIAL**. "Usuarios de especial relevancia" (UER) must meet **all** of:
  - Income: **≥€300,000 gross** in previous calendar year from activity on video-sharing platforms (Art. 3);
  - Audience: **≥1,000,000 followers on one platform** at some point in previous year **or ≥2,000,000 aggregated** across platforms (Art. 4.1.a);
  - Volume: **≥24 videos** in previous year (Art. 4.1.b).
  - Must register in the **Registro Estatal de Prestadores de Servicios de Comunicación Audiovisual** (Art. 39 Ley 13/2022); initial window 2 months from entry into force. UERs are then subject to Ley General de Comunicación Audiovisual advertising/minor-protection rules.
- Below the thresholds, general advertising law (Ley General de Publicidad, Ley de Competencia Desleal) and the AUTOCONTROL/AEA **Código de Conducta sobre el uso de influencers en la publicidad** (2021) apply — **not verified in this run**.
- No official count of registered UERs found.

**Market**: IAB Spain/PwC: influencer ad investment **~€158m in 2025, +25.9%**; InfoAdex: ~€129m (+23.5%); IAB Spain/Primetag: €245m (2025) vs €165m (2024). — via puromarketing.com / emprendedores.es (search digest). **INDUSTRY-RESEARCH**; methodologies differ.

**Language**: Spanish dominates; observation: English-only not viable for Spanish consumers; Spanish content also unlocks LatAm creators (cross-market leverage).

**Opportunity (observation)**: Fastest-growing of the big-4 by IAB/PwC measure; threshold-based regime means most mid-tier campaigns face only general ad law + self-regulation. Lower cost base.

### 2.6 Ireland

## 4. Cross-cutting takeaways for a non-EU (India) agency (observations)

## Feasibility for an Indian agency (analysis)

- **Legal surface is large and shifting.** Erid marking with ORD/ERIR reporting, the 3% levy, the blogger registry, the bans on Meta, VPN and restricted platforms, and a pending Telegram/YouTube ban together mean that most global creator channels (Instagram, YouTube, and in practice Telegram) are illegal or legally uncertain ad venues. What remains is mostly Russia-native: VK, Rutube, Dzen and MAX. Kudozz has no presence on these, and access to them would need Russian legal entities and Russian-localised data.
- **Liability for advertisers can be criminal** (the "financing extremism" risk with Meta), not only administrative.
- **Sanctions exposure is indirect but real.** India imposes no sanctions, but any UK-person staff, EU-person staff, UK or EU contractors, or Western platforms and payment rails (Stripe, PayPal, Google, Meta) that Kudozz relies on bring UK and EU services bans and US secondary-sanctions risk into play. Indian banks are reportedly cautious.
- **Data localisation (1 Jul 2025)** conflicts with an India-hosted stack.
- **Reputational:** Kudozz targets India and GCC brands. Russia-facing commercial content invites association risk with Western partners and platforms. Meta, Google and Stripe are core tools for Kudozz.

## Recommendation: **No commercial landing page. At most, research-only content, and preferably none for now.**

Reasons:
1. A commercial "Influencer marketing in Russia" page would solicit services that Kudozz cannot lawfully or practically deliver. The main channels are banned or under pending bans, and delivery requires erid/ORD reporting, levy payment, blogger-registry checks and Russian data localisation.
2. Offering advertising or PR services to Russian entities is directly prohibited for UK persons and, on PR (and likely advertising, unverified), for EU persons. It also exposes Kudozz's Western platform and payment relationships.
3. The rules are changing month to month: the Telegram/YouTube ban enforcement is unresolved as of 10 Nov 2026. Evergreen SEO content would go stale quickly and could mislead readers.
4. If anything is published, limit it to a dated, neutral **regulatory explainer** that lists sources and states "Kudozz does not offer campaigns in Russia". It must contain no CTA, no service offer and no circumvention guidance (no VPN mentions beyond noting the ad ban). Get a lawyer's review before publishing. The lower-risk default is **no Russia content**.
