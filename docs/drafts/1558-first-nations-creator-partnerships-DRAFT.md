# DRAFT, NOT PUBLISHED: Partnering With First Nations Australian Creators

**Status:** Held for paid review by a qualified First Nations expert before publication (topic 1558). Don't publish without that review. The brief asked for it, and a page on this topic written without First Nations input risks giving generic or wrong guidance about many distinct communities.

**Proposed URL once approved:** `/blog/first-nations-creator-partnerships-australia`

**Primary query:** working with First Nations creators (brand partnerships, Australia)

**Reader:** brand and agency teams planning paid partnerships with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander creators.

## What the reviewer should check

1. Whether the framing, terminology and examples are appropriate, and whether anything should be removed.
2. That the page never presents one set of protocols as applying to all communities.
3. Which First Nations-led organisations, consultants or agencies the page should point brands to. The reviewer chooses these; we don't.
4. Whether the payment and attribution guidance is fair.
5. Whether to cite the True Tracks® principles. They are licensed by Terri Janke and Company, so reproducing them needs permission.

## Draft outline

1. **Direct answer.** Work with First Nations creators as paid creative partners, on their terms. Don't use culture as a campaign theme. Get informed consent for any cultural content. Respect Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property (ICIP), attribute properly, pay fairly, and follow the guidance of the relevant community and Traditional Custodians rather than generic rules.
2. **Diversity.** Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples comprise many distinct nations, languages and cultures. Protocols vary, so ask; don't assume.
3. **Creators first, not culture as content.** Brief First Nations creators the same way as any creator, for their audience and craft. Don't brief them only for NAIDOC Week or Reconciliation Week, and don't expect them to educate audiences for free.
4. **ICIP and consent.**
   - Creative Australia's Protocols for using First Nations Cultural and Intellectual Property in the Arts note that Australian law doesn't fully protect traditional symbols, songs, dances and stories.
   - Treat the protocols as best practice: consent, attribution and benefit sharing.
   - Attribution of ICIP custodians is separate from, and additional to, attribution of the creator (Creative Workplaces guidance).
   - Don't ask creators to waive moral rights.
5. **Contracts.** Cover:
   - fair fees
   - specific usage rights, with no perpetual rights to cultural material
   - the creator's approval of edits
   - takedown if cultural concerns arise
   - extra time and budget for community consultation where cultural material is involved
6. **Places and Country.** Filming on Country may need permits and Traditional Owners' permission (for example, the Uluṟu-Kata Tjuṯa media guidelines).
7. **Measurement and reporting.** Report results to the creator. Avoid tokenistic metrics.
8. **Common mistakes:**
   - one-off campaigns timed to awareness weeks
   - using art or language without permission
   - treating one creator as speaking for all communities
   - unpaid consultation
9. **Next step.** Engage First Nations-led advisers early. *The reviewer supplies recommended organisations.*
